Understanding the population, identity and demographic history of Indigenous communities in Tripura through reliable census records.

1. Why Census Records Matter

Census records help us understand population change, the distribution of communities, language use, literacy, livelihoods and the social history of Tripura.

For Tiprasa heritage, census information can be an important resource for studying Indigenous population history and the circumstances of different Scheduled Tribe communities.

However, cultural identity and official census categories are not always identical. The word Tiprasa can express a broader cultural identity, while the Indian census records people through specific administrative categories, including individual Scheduled Tribe entries.

2. Tripura Census: 2011 Reference

The Census of India 2011 is an important official baseline for studying Tripura's population. The state's total population was 3,673,917.

3,673,917 Total population of Tripura, 2011
1,166,813 Scheduled Tribe population of Tripura, 2011
31.8% Approximate share of the state's population recorded as Scheduled Tribes in 2011

These are historical figures from the 2011 Census, not current population estimates. The Scheduled Tribe figure represents the census category as officially defined; it should not automatically be treated as a count of every person who identifies as Tiprasa.

Reference: Census of India 2011, Tripura population and Scheduled Tribe census tables.

3. Understanding the Word “Tiprasa”

Tiprasa is an important identity term associated with Indigenous people of Tripura. Cultural identity can include language, family heritage, oral tradition, community relationships, customary practices and historical memory.

Census classifications serve a different purpose: they allow government statisticians to collect and publish information using defined categories. A cultural identity may encompass experiences and relationships that are not fully represented by one statistical table.

Research principle: When presenting Tiprasa population history, clearly identify whether a figure refers to all Scheduled Tribes, a specific officially listed tribe, a language group or a broader cultural identity.

4. Scheduled Tribe Communities in Tripura

Census of India tables separately list a range of Scheduled Tribe communities in Tripura. The official terminology includes entries such as Tripura/Tripuri/Tippera, Jamatia, Riang, Noatia/Murashing, Chakma, Halam and others.

These names are reproduced here as census classifications. Community names, subgroup classifications and preferred self-identifications may vary by historical period and context.

5. District-Wise Population Research

District-level census tables help researchers examine where Scheduled Tribe communities live and how population patterns differ between rural and urban areas.

For accurate comparisons, use the district boundaries and administrative structure applicable to the census year. Tripura's district structure has changed over time, so older district figures may not be directly comparable with newer administrative boundaries.

  • Compare the total population with the Scheduled Tribe population.
  • Check rural and urban figures separately.
  • Record the census year and the table identifier.
  • Use official district-level tables rather than unsourced social media estimates.

6. Kokborok and Census Language Data

Language statistics are useful for understanding the linguistic heritage of Tripura. The 2011 Census provides a table on Scheduled Tribe populations by mother tongue, including the census language label “Kokbarak” under the Tripuri language grouping.

Language data must be interpreted according to the census table's terminology and definitions. A mother-tongue count is not the same as a count of all people who understand, speak or culturally identify with a language.

The continued use of Kokborok in families, education, literature and community life is an important part of preserving Tiprasa cultural heritage.

7. Historical Population Change

The demographic history of Tripura includes changes associated with political transitions, migration, displacement, economic development and the growth of towns and public institutions. Census records can help researchers study these changes over time.

Census figures alone cannot explain every cause of population change. They should be read alongside historical documents, scholarly research, land records and oral histories from Indigenous communities and other residents.

A responsible account should recognise the rights and dignity of all communities while documenting the particular historical experiences and concerns of Indigenous Tiprasa people.

8. How to Read Census Figures Correctly

  • Year: Always state the census year.
  • Population category: Specify whether the number refers to the entire population, Scheduled Tribes, a particular tribe or a language group.
  • Geography: State whether the figure covers Tripura as a whole, a district, or a town.
  • Source: Link to the original government table.
  • Interpretation: Separate recorded statistics from historical explanations or community perspectives.

10. Preserving Tiprasa Demographic Heritage

Census records are more than numbers. When preserved and interpreted carefully, they help future generations study population history, language, education, livelihoods and the changing circumstances of their communities.

Combining official statistics with family memories, community histories and Kokborok cultural records can help build a fuller understanding of Tripura's past and present.
