1. Purpose of This Archive

An archive preserves evidence of the past so that present and future generations can learn from it. Every family photograph, historical document, manuscript and carefully recorded oral history can contribute to our understanding of heritage.

This section of Advocate Mangal Debbarma & Family Heritage provides a starting point for documenting family history, Tiprasa traditions, Kokborok language, the history of Tripura and other subjects connected with our cultural inheritance.

Materials published here should be described accurately, with dates and sources wherever possible. Historical tradition, personal memory and independently verified evidence should be identified separately.

4. Family History and Genealogical Records

Family genealogy connects names and dates with the lives, relationships and experiences of earlier generations. A useful family archive can include:

  • Names of ancestors and documented family relationships.
  • Birth, marriage and death records where publication is appropriate.
  • Village and place names associated with family history.
  • Old photographs with identified people, approximate dates and the source of the photograph.
  • Education, professional service, awards and community contributions supported by records.
  • Oral histories recorded with the permission of the narrator.
Accuracy matters: If a family relationship or historical date has not been confirmed, label it as unverified rather than presenting it as established fact.

5. How to Catalogue a Historical Record

Each archive item should have a short description so that researchers can understand what it is and where it came from.

  • Title: A short, descriptive name.
  • Date: The known date, approximate date or “date unknown”.
  • Creator: The author, photographer, issuing authority or known source.
  • Origin: Where the item came from, if known.
  • Description: What the item records and why it may be historically significant.
  • Evidence: Note whether information is documented, remembered orally or still being researched.
  • Permission: Record consent or copyright information where applicable.

6. Preserving Old Photographs and Documents

  • Keep original papers in a dry, clean and secure place.
  • Scan photographs and documents at a suitable resolution.
  • Use descriptive filenames rather than vague names such as “photo1”.
  • Keep a backup copy in a separate secure location.
  • Do not alter an original scan in a way that changes its historical meaning.
  • Do not publicly upload identity numbers, private addresses, signatures or sensitive family documents.
  • Obtain permission before publishing other people's photographs, personal stories or private records.

7. Research Ethics and Historical Responsibility

Historical research should respect the dignity of living people, Indigenous communities, families and the descendants of those whose experiences are documented.

When discussing migration, land, identity or historical conflict, provide sources and distinguish evidence from interpretation. Avoid unsupported allegations and do not treat the actions of individuals as representative of an entire community.

Personal documents should be shared only when the owner has authority to publish them. Sensitive records may be better preserved privately or described in a catalogue without displaying the document itself.

8. Building This Archive Over Time

This is an evolving research collection. New records can be added gradually after their descriptions, dates, sources and permissions have been checked.

The long-term goal is to preserve a responsible record of family ancestry, Tiprasa heritage, Kokborok, Tripura's history and the documents that help future generations study the past.

This website is a personal educational heritage project. It is not an official government archive or a substitute for professional archival services.
